title: Why I’m building Sunny
date: 2026-10-12

My grandmother’s house was not a place I visited for summer vacation. My grandmother is my home.

For as long as I can remember, I’ve been called ammumma kutty. Ammumma’s baby. She raised me. So much of who I am comes from her.

In 7th standard, I would get so stressed about exams that I’d cry while studying. Ammumma would hold me and sing bhajans while I cried-studied.

I haven’t ironed clothes a day in my life. My school uniforms would appear on my chair, pressed and folded. Lunch made. Lunch box packed. All Ammumma. My job was to study, and she made sure I could do just that.

Sometimes I feel guilty about how much I took for granted. I knew she did all these things. I don’t think I understood how much work went into making my life that easy.

Some of my happiest childhood memories are of getting our work done early, dressing up and going to the temple together. We would spend hours there.

I don’t know anyone who works harder than her. Even at 80, her day starts at 4 am and ends at 11 pm. She waits for my call every day.

But as she’s gotten older, she’s become a little more dependent. Small things. The TV’s HDMI cable connected wrong. A phone bill left unpaid, so there’s no internet. Each time, she needs someone else to help her sort it out.

I want her to have a way to get these things done herself. To decide what she wants to do and be able to do it.

Our parents and grandparents spent decades running entire households. They raised us, managed money, knew every shopkeeper and had someone to call for everything. Now so many ordinary things require an app, a password, an OTP or a form in English.

Knowing what you want is no longer enough to get it done. You also need to know how to use the app, have the account, navigate the payment. So you call your child.

And even when that child is happy to help, your plans depend on when they’re free. A visit to a friend waits. A repair waits. Sometimes you put off asking altogether.

The moment you depend on someone for small things, life gets a little smaller.

That’s why I’m building Sunny.

Sunny is a personal assistant for parents and grandparents. I’m building it so they can ask for what they need, in the language they speak at home, and have it taken care of. Order the usual vegetables. Book an auto to a friend’s house. Follow up with the physio who didn’t come. Arrange for someone to go with them to the doctor.

I don’t think Ammumma gets enough credit for everything she has done. I want people to know about the woman who raised me. And I want her, and other ammummas, to keep deciding how they live.

She has spent her life making things possible for me. I want to build something that makes more things possible for her.
